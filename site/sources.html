Sources and credits

Barcode format

  • GB/T 9185-2003, Photography — 135-size film and magazine — Specifications, China’s identical adoption of ISO 1007:2000: section 7 for the edge barcode, section 9 for the cassette barcode and Annex A for DX numbers. Wikipedia’s DX number and DX encoding articles summarize ISO 1007.
  • Olliver, Johnson & Patton (Eastman Kodak), US patent 4,965,628, “Photographic film with latent image multi-field bar code and eye-readable symbols”, 1990.
  • Fuji Photo Film, US patent 5,122,645, describing the original layout with seven clock bars.
  • The DX Film Edge reader in zxing-cpp and the generator in Zint by Antoine Mérino, used to cross-check every rule of the decoder; Zint’s test vectors are part of this project’s tests.
  • Antoine Mérino, Identifying the manufacturer of your 35mm (and APS) films, 2024.
  • Aurélien Lawley, Decoding 35mm DX film edge barcodes.
  • alibosworth, DX Latent Edge Code — Format Reference.

Film data

  • International Imaging Industry Association, DX Codes for 135-Size Film, February 2008: Part 1 assignments and the product list, transcribed as factual data. View the document.
  • The Big Film Database (dxdatabase) © 2018 by Dxdatabase, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0. Adapted: only entries with a DX number are kept, the image-name column is removed, “#” search tags are split from names and HTML markup is removed. Thanks also to Antoine Mérino’s modified edition.

This tool

Free software under the GNU GPL v3. Source code, tests and data scripts are on GitHub. Everything runs in your browser, and photos never leave your device. The code on the strip is kept in the page’s address, which the site receives only when you reload the page or open a link to it.
